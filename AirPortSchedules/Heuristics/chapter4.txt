4.1 INTRODUCTION 
Decisions pertaining to the strategic and tactical level aspects might not be dynamic 
in nature. Nevertheless, decisions have to be taken within a reasonable amount of 
time. Owing to the NP-complete nature of the problem, we saw in the previous 
chapter that exact methodologies fail to arrive at an optimum solution within 
computational times deemed practical. In this chapter, we propose two heuristics to 
solve the integrated LAP. The first heuristic is designed based on 
algorithm whereas the second heuristic uses ant colony optimization (ACO)-based 
approach to arrive at a solution. 
4.2 
 
4.2.1 Input Parameters 
We design the heuristic as a series of least cost path problems for each chosen 
locomotive that needs to be solved in such a way that each locomotive serves as 
many trains as possible and at the same time the total number of locomotives is also 
minimized. To solve the problems as lowest cost path problem, the arcs in the 
network need to have costs associated with them. Since not all arcs have costs or 
distance associated with them, there is a need for proxy costs. Hence, we assign 
proxy costs to all arcs.  

78 
 
The priority is to ensure that each locomotive serves as many train arcs as possible. 
Accordingly, TrArcs are assigned the least cost. These arcs are assigned costs equal 
to the ranks of actual costs when they are arranged in ascending order of their actual 
costs. Once serviced by a locomotive, the costs of TrArcs are increased marginally 
but higher than the cost of other un-serviced TrArcs. This is to ensure that the 
possibility of selection of such TrArcs for deadhauling of locomotive remains open. 
Once two locomotives serve any TrArc, the cost is increased to infinity to avoid the 
selection of such TrArcs. 
Each TrArc is preceeded and succeeded by CDArcs. They are assigned the next 
lowest cost to ensure that whenever a TrArcs is chosen, other incoming/ outgoing 
Arcs are avoided.  
Waiting Arcs at the shed and at stations are assigned costs which are proportional 
to the duration of the time (difference between arrival time and departure time of 
the arc). 
Deadheading arcs originating either at the shed or stations but terminating only at 
a station are assigned the next lowest cost. Deadheading arcs terminating at the 
sheds are given very high costs so as to avoid locomotive trips to sheds unless 
absolutely necessary. Such cases include situations when the locomotive is due for 
periodic maintenance, the locomotive trip has reached the end of the planning 
horizon or when a direct link between two stations is absent and the locomotive has 
to take a detour through a locomotive shed to reach some other station where a train 
is waiting to be serviced.

Trip maintenance and periodic maintenance arcs are given the highest costs. Since 
the algorithm needs to avoid these arcs unless the locomotives are due for 
maintenance. 
4.2.2 Locomotive Selection 
Depending on its type, hp rating and top speed, the procurement cost of each 
locomotive varies. Capital investment represents the major cost in our objective 
function. Further, depending on the route and distance travelled, the operating cost 
associated with each locomotive varies while servicing any particular TrArc or 
DArc or D`Arc. This cost also depends on the age of the locomotive as older 
locomotives tend to consume more power/fuel depending on whether it is electric/ 
diesel and hence are prone to be less efficient. We also assign penalty costs to 
locomotives servicing certain TrArcs when a particular type of locomotive is 
preferred less/ not preferred to service the corresponding TrArc. Given this set of 
costs, we need to select one locomotive at a time which will service as many TrArcs 
as possible, while adhering to the constraints and return back to its home shed at 
the end of the planning horizon. We calculate the total cost of each locomotive by 
adding the respective annuitized capital investment cost to the operating costs and 
penalty cost of all the arcs. We then use a greedy approach of selecting the 
locomotive with the lowest total cost first. Once the locomotive has served a set of 
TrArcs as allotted by the algorithm, we replace the cost with infinity and then 
choose the second lowest cost locomotive.

4.3 ANT COLONY OPTIMIZATION BASED HEURISTIC 
Ant Colony optimization is a technique for optimization that was introduced in 
establishing the shortest path between food sources and nests and this technique is 
applied on number of combinatorial optimization problem. In this section, we 
design an ACO based algorithm to solve the LAP.  
4.3.1 Input Parameters 
TrArcs, DArcs and DlArcs are the only that have costs and distance associated with 
them. As a result, there is a need for proxy costs. We assign proxy costs to all arcs. 
The priority is to ensure that each locomotive serves as many train arcs as possible. 
To ensure this, we need to ensure that TrArcs have the maximum amount of 
pheromones and consequently TrArcs are assigned the least cost. These arcs 
assigned costs equal to the ranks of actual costs when they are arranged in 
ascending order of their actual costs. Once serviced by a locomotive, the costs of 
TrArcs are increased to a level higher than the cost of other un-serviced TrArcs. 
This ensures that the un-serviced TrArcs will still have a higher probability of 
selection on account of higher pheromone levels, but the possibility of selection of 
serviced TrArcs for deadhauling of locomotive remains open as well. Once, 2 
locomotives serve any TrArc, the cost is increasing the cost to infinity is not 
sufficient to avoid the selection of such TrArcs.
