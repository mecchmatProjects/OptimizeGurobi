Building on the corrected ground model of Chapter~\ref{ch:ground}, this
chapter incorporates maintenance feasibility into the compact tail-assignment
formulation. The assignment variables determine where each aircraft is during
the planning horizon, but they do not state whether a required check can be
performed at that location or whether the aircraft has exceeded its allowable
exposure since the preceding check. The maintenance layer therefore couples
three decisions: the flight after which a check is initiated, the day on which
the check is recorded, and the cumulative utilisation that the check resets.

The presentation begins with one hours-based type-A check, following the
maintenance extension of \citet{khaled2018compact}. This isolates the logic of
the cumulative-hour restriction and the correction of Constraint~(13). The
four-level A/B/C/D hierarchy, check subsumption, and multi-day heavy checks are
introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:hierarchy}.

\section{Scope and operational assumptions}
\label{sec:maintenance}

The maintenance model inherits all flight coverage, airport continuity, turn
time, and non-overlap assumptions of Chapter~\ref{ch:ground}. In addition, it
uses the following assumptions.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A type-A check is required before cumulative block time exceeds the
    threshold $T_{\max}^{A}$.
    \item A check can be initiated only after an aircraft arrives at a
    maintenance-capable airport.
    \item A check consumes one unit of airport maintenance capacity on its
    scheduled day.
    \item In the single-check model, at most one check is performed on an
    aircraft on a given day.
    \item A triggered overnight check prevents the aircraft from operating a
    later flight on the same day. Longer check durations are treated in
    Chapter~\ref{ch:hierarchy}.
    \item The state entering the horizon is known: the initial airport and the
    cumulative hours $\Phi_j^A$ since the last type-A check are input data.
\end{enumerate}

The day index records maintenance opportunities; the flight timetable remains
continuous in absolute time. Consequently, an arrival must be both spatially
eligible and temporally compatible with a check. Merely ending a day at an
arbitrary airport is insufficient.

\section{Sets, parameters, and auxiliary definitions}

Let $\D=\{1,\ldots,n_d\}$ be the ordered set of planning days and
$\MA\subseteq\A$ the set of maintenance-capable airports. For each flight
$i$, let $d_i^{\uparrow}$ and $d_i^{\downarrow}$ be its departure and arrival
days, $\tilde t_i$ its block time, and $a_i^{\downarrow}$ its arrival airport.
Define
\begin{equation}
    \F^{\downarrow}(m)
    =\{i\in\F:a_i^{\downarrow}=m\},
    \qquad m\in\MA,
    \label{eq:maint_eligible_arrivals}
\end{equation}
and let
\begin{equation}
    \F^{+}(d,i)
    =\{i'\in\F:d_{i'}^{\uparrow}=d,
      \ t_{i'}^{\uparrow}>t_i^{\downarrow}\}
    \label{eq:maint_later_flights}
\end{equation}
be the flights departing later on day $d$ than the arrival of flight $i$.
Only departures that are otherwise compatible with aircraft $j$ need to be
included when the inequalities are generated.

For $d<d'$, denote by
\begin{equation}
    F_{d,d'}
    =\{i\in\F:d<d_i^{\uparrow}<d'\}
    \label{eq:maint_day_interval}
\end{equation}
the flights whose departure days lie strictly inside the day pair. This
open-interval convention separates flights accumulated between the two
boundary-day reset decisions. Other endpoint conventions are possible, but
the set definition and reset semantics must be used consistently.

\section{Maintenance decision variables}

For every maintenance-eligible arrival, aircraft, and feasible check day,
define
\begin{equation}
    z_{ijd}=
    \begin{cases}
          1, & \substack{\text{if aircraft $j$ initiates a type-A check after flight $i$}\\
                              \text{and the check is recorded on day $d$},}\\
        0, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:maint_trigger_variable}
\end{equation}
The variable is created only when $i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)$ for some
$m\in\MA$ and when day $d$ is compatible with the arrival and check duration.
This sparse domain is preferable to creating impossible variables and fixing
them to zero.

The daily aggregate variable is
\begin{equation}
    y_{jd}=
    \begin{cases}
        1, & \text{if aircraft $j$ undergoes a type-A check on day $d$},\\
        0, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:maint_day_variable}
\end{equation}
The distinction is important: $z_{ijd}$ identifies the arrival and therefore
the maintenance airport, whereas $y_{jd}$ is the reset indicator used in the
spacing and cumulative-hour constraints.

\section{Extended objective function}

The basic assignment objective is augmented by the cost of selected
maintenance events:
\begin{equation}
    \min\quad
    \sum_{i\in\F}\sum_{j\in\Pc}c_{ij}x_{ij}
    +\sum_{m\in\MA}\sum_{i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)}
      \sum_{j\in\Pc}\sum_{d\in\D}mc_{ijd}z_{ijd}.
    \label{eq:maintenance_objective}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $mc_{ijd}$ may combine labour, station usage, overnight
parking, and opportunity cost. A maintenance-ineligible assignment must be
excluded by the variable domain or constraints; a large objective coefficient
does not make it infeasible.

\section{Maintenance-event feasibility}

\subsection{C8: blocking subsequent same-day flights}

If maintenance begins after flight $i$, aircraft $j$ cannot operate a later
flight while the check is active. For the overnight type-A abstraction this is
written as
\begin{equation}
    z_{ijd}+x_{i'j}\leq1,
    \qquad
    \substack{\forall i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m),\ m\in\MA,\\
    \forall i'\in\F^{+}(d,i),\ j\in\Pc,\ d\in\D.}
    \label{eq:maint_c8}
\end{equation}
When the precise check duration is available, $\F^{+}(d,i)$ can be replaced
by the departures whose times intersect the maintenance interval. The
essential implication remains the same: selecting $z_{ijd}$ removes every
flight that would make the check physically impossible.

\subsection{C9: coupling a check to its trigger flight}

A check can be attached to flight $i$ only if that flight is assigned to the
same aircraft:
\begin{equation}
    z_{ijd}\leq x_{ij},
    \qquad
    \forall(i,j,d)\text{ in the domain of }z.
    \label{eq:maint_c9}
\end{equation}
If several feasible check days are associated with the same arrival, the
stronger aggregated form $\sum_d z_{ijd}\leq x_{ij}$ prevents that arrival
from triggering more than one check.

\subsection{C10: airport-day maintenance capacity}

All checks initiated at airport $m$ on day $d$ share its finite capacity:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)}\sum_{j\in\Pc}z_{ijd}
    \leq\operatorname{mcap}_{md},
    \qquad \forall m\in\MA,\ d\in\D.
    \label{eq:maint_c10}
\end{equation}
This is a resource constraint rather than an aircraft-feasibility constraint:
each selected trigger can be individually valid while their simultaneous use
of the same station is infeasible.

\subsection{C11 and C14: daily aggregation and uniqueness}

The trigger and reset representations are linked by
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{m\in\MA}\sum_{i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)}z_{ijd}=y_{jd},
    \qquad \forall j\in\Pc,\ d\in\D.
    \label{eq:maint_c11}
\end{equation}
Because $y_{jd}$ is binary, equality~\eqref{eq:maint_c11} already permits at
most one trigger for aircraft $j$ on day $d$. The explicit inequality
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{m\in\MA}\sum_{i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)}z_{ijd}\leq1,
    \qquad \forall j\in\Pc,\ d\in\D,
    \label{eq:maint_c14}
\end{equation}
is therefore redundant in the single-type model but documents the operational
rule and becomes useful when several check types share a day.

\subsection{C12: maximum calendar spacing in the final hierarchy}

\textit{The original single-type exposition uses a calendar spacing rule for
the A-check. In the implemented A/B/C/D model, however, A and B are regulated
by corrected cumulative flight-hour constraints, whereas C and D are regulated
by calendar-day spacing. Accordingly, the operational C12 rule is stated for
$c\in\{C,D\}$ and uses the hierarchy indicator $\gamma_{jrc}$.} For every
window containing $d_{\max}^{c}$ consecutive days, at least one qualifying
check must occur:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{r=d}^{d+d_{\max}^{c}-1}\gamma_{jrc}\geq1,
    \qquad
    \substack{\forall j\in\Pc,\\
    c\in\{C,D\},\\
    d=1,\ldots,n_d-d_{\max}^{c}+1.}
    \label{eq:maint_c12}
\end{equation}
\textit{This sliding-window form is equivalent to requiring that no run of
$d_{\max}^{c}$ days is free of a check at level at least $c$. It does not
replace the A/B flight-hour constraints: an aircraft may accumulate its hour
limit early in a permissible calendar window.}

\section{Original cumulative-hour constraint}

For aircraft $j$ and day pair $(d,d')$, define
\begin{equation}
    H_{jdd'}=\sum_{i\in F_{d,d'}}\tilde t_i x_{ij},
    \qquad
    Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}=\sum_{r=d+1}^{d'-1}y_{jr}.
    \label{eq:maint_interval_hours}
\end{equation}
Here $Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}$ counts checks strictly inside the interval. The
original formulation of \citet{khaled2018compact} uses the single inequality
\begin{equation}
    H_{jdd'}\leq T_{\max}^{A}
    +M_{dd'}(2-y_{jd}-y_{jd'})
    +M_{dd'}Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}.
    \label{eq:c13_original}
\end{equation}
It is generated for the admissible day pairs, typically
$2\leq d'-d\leq d_{\max}^{A}-1$.

The intended logic is to enforce the hour limit on an interval without an
intervening reset and to relax it when a check splits the accumulation period.
The endpoint expression does not implement that logic for all endpoint
patterns. If $Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}=0$ and exactly one endpoint has a check, then
\eqref{eq:c13_original} becomes
\begin{equation}
    H_{jdd'}\leq T_{\max}^{A}+M_{dd'},
    \label{eq:c13_original_one_endpoint}
\end{equation}
which is non-binding for a valid big-$M$. If neither endpoint has a check, the
available slack increases to $2M_{dd'}$. Thus the original row controls only
the endpoint pattern $(y_{jd},y_{jd'})=(1,1)$ when the interior is check-free.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint activation defect]
    \label{lem:c13_endpoint_defect}
    Suppose $Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}=0$ and $M_{dd'}$ is at least the maximum
    attainable value of $H_{jdd'}$. If either endpoint indicator is zero,
    Constraint~\eqref{eq:c13_original} cannot exclude an assignment with
    $H_{jdd'}>T_{\max}^{A}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If exactly one endpoint is zero, the right-hand side is
    $T_{\max}^{A}+M_{dd'}$; if both are zero, it is
    $T_{\max}^{A}+2M_{dd'}$. In either case it is no smaller than every
    attainable value of $H_{jdd'}$, so the row cannot enforce the threshold.
\end{proof}

For example, let $T_{\max}^{A}=60$, $M_{12}=200$, and let the assigned flights
accumulate $150$ minutes over the interval. With
$(y_{j1},y_{j2})=(0,1)$ and no interior check, the original inequality reads
$150\leq260$ and accepts the overrun.

\section{Corrected endpoint-anchored formulation}
\label{sec:c13_correction}

The correction replaces the combined endpoint expression by two inequalities,
one anchored at each boundary:
\begin{align}
    H_{jdd'}
    &\leq T_{\max}^{A}
    +M_{dd'}Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}+M_{dd'}y_{jd},
    \label{eq:c13a}\\
    H_{jdd'}
    &\leq T_{\max}^{A}
    +M_{dd'}Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}+M_{dd'}y_{jd'}.
    \label{eq:c13b}
\end{align}
The pair is generated for every aircraft and every admissible day pair. The
first row is relaxed by a check at the start boundary; the second is relaxed
by a check at the end boundary. An interior check relaxes both because the
interval then contains a reset and must be analysed through shorter windows.

Table~\ref{tab:c13_activation} makes the binary endpoint logic explicit for an
interval with no interior check.

    \centering
    \small
    \caption{Activation of the original and corrected C13 rows when
    $Y_{jdd'}^{\circ}=0$. ``Active'' means that $H_{jdd'}\leq T_{\max}^{A}$.}
    \label{tab:c13_activation}
    \begin{tabular}{cccc}
        \toprule
        $y_{jd}$ & $y_{jd'}$ & Original row & Corrected pair \\
        \midrule
        0 & 0 & Relaxed by $2M_{dd'}$ & Both active \\

        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}

The table also clarifies the modelling convention behind the correction.
Endpoint indicators act as reset relaxers for their respective anchored rows;
the corrected pair is designed to control check-free windows and one-sided
opening or closing windows. It should not be justified by claiming that each
new row dominates the original row for every fixed binary endpoint pattern.
Its validity follows from generating all admissible windows together with C12
and the opening-horizon condition below.

\begin{proposition}[Check-free-window enforcement]
    \label{prop:c13_check_free_windows}
    For any admissible interval with no interior check and at least one
    check-free endpoint, Constraints~\eqref{eq:c13a}--\eqref{eq:c13b} impose
    $H_{jdd'}\leq T_{\max}^{A}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    If $y_{jd}=0$, the big-$M$ endpoint term vanishes from
    \eqref{eq:c13a}; if $y_{jd'}=0$, it vanishes from
    \eqref{eq:c13b}. Since the interior sum is zero, either applicable row
    reduces to $H_{jdd'}\leq T_{\max}^{A}$. If both endpoints are zero, both
    rows reduce to that inequality.
\end{proof}

The numerical example above is rejected by the start-anchored row:
\begin{equation}
    150\leq60+200(0)+200(0)=60,
    \label{eq:c13_counterexample_corrected}
\end{equation}
which is false. The correction therefore closes precisely the one-endpoint
case admitted by the original formulation.

\section{Pre-horizon accumulated hours}

Without an opening condition, an aircraft entering the horizon close to its
limit is incorrectly treated as newly maintained. Let $F_{0,d'}$ contain the
flights departing strictly before boundary day $d'$. The remaining
allowance before the first in-horizon check is
$T_{\max}^{A}-\Phi_j^A$, giving
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i\in F_{0,d'}}\tilde t_i x_{ij}
    \leq T_{\max}^{A}-\Phi_j^A
    +M_{0d'}\sum_{r=1}^{d'-1}y_{jr}
    +M_{0d'}y_{jd'},
    \qquad \forall j\in\Pc,\ d'\in\D.
    \label{eq:c13_initial_hours}
\end{equation}
Before the first check, all $y$ terms vanish and the residual allowance is
enforced. Once a check occurs, the pre-horizon counter is reset and the row is
relaxed. This constraint is essential whenever $\Phi_j^A>0$; omitting it can
make an apparently feasible schedule violate the true maintenance state on
its first day.

\section{Choice of the big-$M$ coefficient}

A global constant equal to the total duration of every flight is valid but
unnecessarily weak. The formulation instead uses a day-pair-specific value
satisfying
\begin{equation}
    M_{dd'}\geq
    \max\left\{
      \sum_{i\in F_{d,d'}}\tilde t_i x_{ij}:
      x\text{ satisfies the corrected ground model for aircraft }j
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:maintenance_big_m}
\end{equation}
A safe inexpensive bound is $\sum_{i\in F_{d,d'}}\tilde t_i$. A tighter value
can be obtained as a longest feasible path in the acyclic flight-connection
graph restricted to the interval. Because every $M_{dd'}$ appears directly in
the LP relaxation, reducing it without sacrificing validity strengthens the
formulation and improves numerical conditioning.

\section{Physical interpretation and model validity}

The maintenance equations do not repair an infeasible flight trajectory; they
assume the corrected ground domain of Chapter~\ref{ch:ground}. In particular,
Constraint~\eqref{eq:ground_overlap} ensures that cumulative hours are measured
on a trajectory that one aircraft can actually execute. The effect of the
ground correction on C8--C14 is therefore a domain correction: the algebra of
the maintenance rows is unchanged, but those rows are no longer evaluated on
simultaneous or overlapping flight assignments.

\begin{theorem}[Feasibility of the single-type maintenance layer]
    \label{thm:maintenance_layer_feasibility}
    Assume that the admissible C12--C13 windows cover every possible
    check-free accumulation span. Any integral solution satisfying the
    corrected ground model, Constraints~\eqref{eq:maint_c8}--
    \eqref{eq:maint_c12}, the endpoint pair~\eqref{eq:c13a}--
    \eqref{eq:c13b}, the opening condition~\eqref{eq:c13_initial_hours}, and
    the variable domains induces physically executable aircraft trajectories
    with capacity-feasible type-A maintenance and no cumulative-hour overrun.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Fix aircraft $j$. The result of Chapter~\ref{ch:ground} orders its assigned
    flights into a turn-feasible, non-overlapping trajectory. By
    \eqref{eq:maint_c9}, each selected trigger belongs to that trajectory; by
    \eqref{eq:maint_c8}, no later same-day assignment conflicts with the
    selected check. Equations~\eqref{eq:maint_c10} and
    \eqref{eq:maint_c11} enforce station capacity and a consistent daily reset.
    Constraint~\eqref{eq:maint_c12} prevents an excessive calendar gap.

    Before the first in-horizon check, \eqref{eq:c13_initial_hours} includes
    the entering exposure $\Phi_j^A$. After a reset, suppose that the aircraft
    accumulated more than $T_{\max}^{A}$ without another check. By the stated
    window-coverage assumption, this span contains an admissible interval with
    no interior check and at least one check-free endpoint. Proposition~
    \ref{prop:c13_check_free_windows} bounds that interval by
    $T_{\max}^{A}$, a contradiction. Thus the maintenance schedule is
    executable and respects the cumulative-hour limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete single-type formulation and size}

The maintenance-extended model consists of the corrected ground objective and
constraints, objective~\eqref{eq:maintenance_objective}, C8--C12 and C14,
the corrected C13 pair, and the opening-horizon rows, with
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}\in\{0,1\},\qquad
    z_{ijd}\in\{0,1\},\qquad
    y_{jd}\in\{0,1\}.
    \label{eq:maintenance_integrality}
\end{equation}
With sparse trigger indexing, the model adds at most
$O(|\F||\Pc||\D|)$ trigger variables, $O(|\Pc||\D|)$ daily variables, and
$O(|\Pc||\D|^2)$ cumulative-hour rows. The day-pair rows are the principal
growth relative to the $O(|\F||\Pc|)$ ground assignment layer.

This chapter establishes the maintenance logic for one hours-based check. The
next chapter replaces $y_{jd}$ by type-specific and hierarchy-aware reset
indicators, distinguishes hours-based A/B checks from calendar-based C/D
checks, and represents checks whose duration extends over multiple days.
