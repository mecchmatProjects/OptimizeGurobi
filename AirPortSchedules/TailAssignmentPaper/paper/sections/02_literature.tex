\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}
%%==========================================================================

\subsection{Mathematical programming formulations}

Tail assignment has been modelled using compact assignment, time-space
network, and set-partitioning formulations. The set-partitioning approach
represents feasible aircraft duties as columns and is commonly solved with
column generation and branch-and-price
\citep{lavoie1988new,minoux1984column,barnhart1998flight,sarac2006branch}.
Multi-commodity flow formulations explicitly represent aircraft movement
between flights \citep{levin1971scheduling}. Time-space networks encode
airport--time events and feasible transitions, and have been applied to fleet
assignment and aircraft routing
\citep{hane1995fleet,clarke1996maintenance,sherali2006airline}. These models
provide alternative representations with different size and decomposition
trade-offs; comparisons require aligned decision scopes and instance data.

Compact assignment formulations seek to retain a small set of flight--aircraft
assignment variables while expressing continuity and operational constraints
through linking inequalities. \citet{khaled2018compact} present such a model
and report computational results on 400 instances generated randomly from a
real data set of a domestic low-cost airline in Asia (1\,580 flight legs
over 30 days, 37 airports, and 50 aircraft). The present paper
uses that formulation as its starting point and focuses on the validity of
its continuity conditions and the finite-horizon treatment of type-A
flight-hour maintenance.

\subsection{Maintenance in aircraft routing}

Maintenance constraints couple aircraft routing decisions with the timing
and location of checks. Prior work embeds maintenance in aircraft-routing
and network models \citep{clarke1997aircraft,barnhart1998flight,
cordeau2001benders,lacasse2010aircraft,liang2011new,liang2012network}.
This paper considers only a single type-A check triggered by accumulated
flight time. Calendar-based checks and interactions among multiple check
types are outside its scope.

\subsection{Position of the present paper}

The contribution is limited to two questions within the compact model:
whether its strict-time balance inequalities alone exclude simultaneous
departures, and how type-A flight-hour limits should be represented when
initial accumulated hours are nonzero.
