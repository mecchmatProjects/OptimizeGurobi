%%==========================================================================
\section{Type-A Maintenance with Opening Exposure}
\label{sec:maintenance}
%%==========================================================================

This section adds a single maintenance type, denoted A, whose limit is
measured in cumulative flight minutes. A check is performed after the day's
flights at an eligible maintenance airport. As in \Cref{tab:notation}, $d_i$
and $\bar d_i$ denote the departure and arrival days of flight $i$,
respectively. Let $\Phi^A_j$ be aircraft $j$'s accumulated flight minutes
since its previous type-A check at the start of the horizon. The limit
$T^A_{\max}$ and $\Phi^A_j$ are measured in the same units.

For each aircraft $j$ and day $d$, define the assigned flight time
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_daily_hours}
  q_{jd} = \sum_{i\in\F: d_i=d} \tilde{t}_i x_{ij},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{t}_i$ is the duration of flight $i$. The binary variable
$z_{ijd}$ indicates that a type-A check is triggered after flight $i$ on day
$d$, and $y_{jd}$ indicates that aircraft $j$ receives a check on day $d$.
The assignment, routing, and pairwise non-overlap constraints from
\Cref{sec:basic} remain in force.

Let $mc_{ijd}$ denote the cost of performing the check after flight $i$ on
day $d$. The objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_obj}
  \min\; \sum_{i\in\F}\sum_{j\in\Pc}c_{ij}x_{ij}
    +\sum_{i\in\F}\sum_{j\in\Pc}\sum_{d\in\D}mc_{ijd}z_{ijd}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Check placement}

Let $\D=\{1,\ldots,n_d\}$ and let $\MA\subseteq\A$ be the set of
maintenance-capable airports. A flight can trigger a check only at its
arrival airport on its arrival day. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_check_candidates}
  \mathcal{E}^A_d
  =\{i\in\F:\ \aI{i}\in\MA,\ \bar d_i=d\},
  \qquad d\in\D.
\end{equation}
In particular, an overnight flight is eligible only on its arrival day,
not on its departure day. To retain the full indexing used in the objective
and linking equations, all ineligible trigger variables are fixed to zero.
The set of flights blocked by an eligible trigger is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_check_later_flights}
  F(d,i)=\{i'\in\F:\ d_{i'}=d,\ \tD{i'}\ge\tI{i}\}.
\end{equation}
The non-strict time comparison also blocks a departure exactly at arrival
when the minimum turn time is zero.

The check-placement constraints link a selected check to an assigned flight,
respect same-day sequencing, and enforce airport capacity:
\begin{align}
  z_{ijd} &= 0,
    && \forall j,d,\;i\in\F\setminus\mathcal{E}^A_d,
       \label{eq:a_check_eligibility}\\
  z_{ijd} &\le x_{ij},
    && \forall i,j,d, \label{eq:a_check_assignment}\\
  z_{ijd}+x_{i'j} &\le 1,
    && \forall j,d,\;i\in\mathcal{E}^A_d,\;i'\in F(d,i),
       \label{eq:a_check_blocking}\\
  \sum_{i\in\F} z_{ijd} &= y_{jd},
    && \forall j,d, \label{eq:a_check_link}\\
  \sum_{i\in\F^{\downarrow}(m)}\sum_{j\in\Pc}z_{ijd}
    &\le \mathrm{mcap}_{m,d},
    && \forall m\in\MA,d. \label{eq:a_check_capacity}
\end{align}
Here $\F^{\downarrow}(m)=\{i\in\F:\ \aI{i}=m\}$. The variables $x$, $z$,
and $y$ are binary. If $y_{jd}=1$, the linking equation selects exactly one
eligible assigned arrival. With positive flight durations, the basic
non-overlap constraints exclude flights overlapping that arrival, and
\eqref{eq:a_check_blocking} excludes all later departures on day $d$.
Aircraft $j$ is therefore at the selected maintenance airport after its
last flight of that day, and the check consumes capacity at that airport
on that same day. Neither an arrival on another day nor an arrival at a
non-maintenance airport can provide a reset. This arrival-trigger convention
does not represent checks on days with no assigned arrival; those would
require a separate ground-location representation.

A check on day $d$ occurs after the flights counted in $q_{jd}$, so those
flights consume the pre-check allowance. These placement constraints establish
the check's location and day, not that a positive-duration check can finish
before the next flight; that requires a separate downtime condition.

\subsection{Establishing maintenance executability: two options}
\label{sec:a_executability}

Two implemented approaches close this timing gap. Option~A encodes
executability explicitly; Option~B certifies a protected-period input class
and omits timing rows made redundant by that certificate. Both retain the same
arrival-trigger convention and the requirement that a check follows the
aircraft's last flight of the day.

For both approaches, specify the check duration $\tau^A_j>0$ and nonnegative
preparation and release buffers $\eta^-_j,\eta^+_j$, all in minutes. The
relationship of these buffers to ordinary turn time must be declared:
the existing turn-time constraints remain applicable, and an overlap between
turnaround work and maintenance must not be assumed implicitly. To make
the daily capacity model sufficient, interpret each unit of
$\mathrm{mcap}_{m,d}$ as a staffed, independent maintenance slot reserved
for that night. A daily throughput quota alone does not guarantee available
bays or crews at the required times; shared time-dependent resources would
instead need explicit resource-scheduling constraints.

\paragraph{Option A: enforce overnight feasibility with constraints.}
Let $[\ell_{md},u_{md}]$ be the available service window at airport $m$
for the night following day $d$, expressed in absolute minutes. The window
may cross midnight but must end during day $d+1$; successive service
windows at the same station must not overlap. The window covers the check
itself; preparation and release buffers describe additional aircraft
unavailability. For an eligible arrival
$i\in\mathcal{E}^A_d$ at $m=\aI{i}$, compute
\begin{align}
  S_{ijd}&=\max\{\tI{i}+\eta^-_j,\ell_{md}\},
    \label{eq:a_overnight_start}\\
  C_{ijd}&=S_{ijd}+\tau^A_j,\qquad
  R_{ijd}=C_{ijd}+\eta^+_j.
    \label{eq:a_overnight_release}
\end{align}
Here $S_{ijd}$ is the earliest start, $C_{ijd}$ the completion time, and
$R_{ijd}$ the release time. The Option~A implementation fixes $z_{ijd}=0$
whenever $C_{ijd}>u_{md}$ and adds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_overnight_conflicts}
  z_{ijd}+x_{i'j}\le1,
  \qquad \forall i'\in\F:\ \tI{i}\le\tD{i'}<R_{ijd},
\end{equation}
for every remaining eligible $(i,j,d)$. These rows apply to departures at
\emph{all} airports and on subsequent days, not only to departures on day
$d$ at the maintenance station. A departure at $R_{ijd}$ is allowed.
The basic non-overlap constraints exclude flights already in progress at
the trigger arrival, while \eqref{eq:a_overnight_conflicts} prevents leaving
before the check and release work finish. The same-day blocking rows remain
in force even if the check finishes early.

Under the reserved-slot interpretation, a continuous service window, and
start-time-independent duration and cost, the earliest start suffices:
delaying a check cannot improve its completion or release deadline. If
these conditions are relaxed, start-time variables and resource constraints
may be necessary. For example, a 23:30 arrival followed by a 01:00 departure
cannot accommodate a three-hour check, even though the flights have
different departure days and may satisfy ordinary turn time. The proposed
rows reject that combination rather than allowing a reset between them.

\begin{proposition}[Option A executability for selected triggers]
  Let $(x,z,y)$ satisfy C1--C5, C9--C11, the Option A fixed trigger-domain
  restrictions, \eqref{eq:a_overnight_conflicts}, and the simultaneous bay
  capacity rows. If a trigger $z_{ijd}=1$, it has a reserved interval
  $[S_{ijd},C_{ijd})$ inside its declared service window, no selected flight
  departs from trigger arrival until $R_{ijd}$, and at most
  $\mathrm{mcap}_{m,d}$ selected intervals overlap at any instant at station
  $m$. Therefore every selected reset is attached to an executable maintenance
  action and its physical completion precedes the next assigned flight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  C9 links the trigger to its assigned flight and C11 selects at most one
  trigger per aircraft-day. Triggers with $C_{ijd}>u_{md}$ or a terminal
  deadline violation are fixed to zero. For a selected trigger, Option A's
  conflict rows forbid every otherwise selected departure with
  $\tI{i}\le\tD{i'}<R_{ijd}$; C1--C5 supply one continuous, non-overlapping
  route for the remaining departures. Simultaneous occupancy is checked at
  every event start, which is sufficient for equal and unequal fixed intervals
  because occupancy can increase only at a start. Thus the release and bay
  conditions hold directly, not as an unstated nightly assumption.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Option B: certify a protected overnight period by assumptions.}
Alternatively, restrict the input timetable and operating policy so that
each night has a common protected period $[\ell_d,u_d]$ contained in every
usable station's service window. Require the following conditions to hold
for every allowed aircraft assignment, not merely for a selected solution:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Flights depart and arrive on the same day. All flights on day $d$
  finish by $\ell_d-\eta^-_j$ for every compatible aircraft $j$, and no
  flight on day $d+1$ departs before $u_d$. Thus no flight crosses the
  protected period.
\item The available period satisfies
  $\tau^A_j+\eta^+_j\le u_d-\ell_d$ for every compatible aircraft.
  A check can start at $\ell_d$ and the aircraft can be released by $u_d$.
\item The reserved maintenance slots and required staff are available
  throughout the protected period, with the same capacity interpretation
  as in Option A.
\item The final protected period is available even when it extends beyond
  the last operating day. Any prescribed latest terminal release time
  $b_j$ satisfies $b_j\ge u_{n_d}$ for each aircraft.
\end{enumerate}
These conditions must be verified from the input data. For any check
selected by the placement rows, arrival and preparation then finish before
$\ell_d$, and maintenance and release finish before the next possible
departure. Hence no additional check--flight timing rows are needed on this
certified class. This is a restrictive scheduling policy, not a
feasible-region-preserving simplification for arbitrary timetables. An
instance that fails the certification lies outside Option B's scope; it
need not be infeasible under Option A.

\paragraph{Reset timing and horizon boundaries.}
Under either option, $y_{jd}$ labels the service night associated with day
$d$, not necessarily the calendar day of completion. The physical hour
counter resets only when the check is completed. Treating this as a reset
between the daily flight blocks is justified only because no assigned
flight can operate between the trigger arrival and release. Option A must
retain the distinct departure and arrival dates of overnight flights;
Option B excludes those flights by assumption. In both cases, specify the
last night's completion deadline even if no later flight appears in the
horizon, and include any required post-horizon aircraft-release commitment.
In Option A, a last-night trigger must also be fixed to zero if
$R_{ij,n_d}>b_j$ when a terminal release deadline $b_j$ is specified.
Option B certifies release by $u_{n_d}\le b_j$.
Otherwise, the absence of a next-flight row could conceal unfinished
maintenance. Checks on days with no assigned arrival remain outside both
proposals unless the trigger representation is extended.

Option A also retains timestamp-level flight-hour rows. For every aircraft $j$
and every candidate flight $i$, let $\mathcal{I}_{ji}$ contain the flights
assigned to $j$ whose arrivals do not exceed $\tI{i}$, and let
$\mathcal{E}_{ji}$ contain check completions no later than $\tI{i}$.
The opening-exposure row is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^A_j+\sum_{r\in\mathcal{I}_{ji}}t_r x_{rj}
  \le T^A_{\max}+M_0\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{ji}}z_e.
  \label{eq:option_a_opening_timestamp}
\end{equation}
For each candidate check event $e$ completed at $C_e\le\tI{i}$, Option A adds
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal{I}_{ji}\\ C_e<\tI{r}}} t_r x_{rj}
  \le T^A_{\max}
    +M_e\left(1-z_e+
      \sum_{\substack{e'\ne e\\ C_e<C_{e'}\le\tI{i}}}z_{e'}\right).
  \label{eq:option_a_event_timestamp}
\end{equation}
If $e$ is the last selected completion before flight $i$, the second row
bounds the exact exposure after that reset. If it is not selected, or a later
reset occurs, the big-$M$ term relaxes the row. These rows are necessary for
cross-midnight flights because a departure later on the arrival day of a
check may occur after its completion, while the historical daily aggregation
can omit that same-day departure from an interval. On the protected same-day
Option B class, the timestamp rows are implied by the separation of the last
flight block, the protected period, and the first next-day departure.

\paragraph{Option A/B projection.}

\begin{proposition}[Equivalent placement projection on certified Option B inputs]
  \label{prop:option_ab_projection}
  Let an input pass the Option B certificate. Use the same assignment cost,
  check cost, A-hour constraints, arrival-day triggers, calendar policy,
  buffers and terminal deadlines in both models. Then every feasible
  $(x,z,y)$ projection of Option B has an executable timing realization in
  Option A, and every feasible Option A placement is feasible in Option B.
  Consequently the two models have the same integer projection onto
  $(x,z,y)$, although their constraint matrices and LP relaxations may differ.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Certification places every compatible flight outside the protected period,
  and every selected trigger arrival reaches its station before the protected
  period after accounting for preparation. Therefore Option A's earliest
  start is exactly the protected-period start. The period contains check and
  release work, the terminal deadline contains the final release, and no
  selected departure can fall inside the Option A release interval. Thus every
  Option A timing conflict row is implied by the certificate and no selected
  Option B placement can violate one. C10 bounds the number of checks on an
  airport-day. Since all certified checks start in the common protected period
  with equal A duration, this count also dominates simultaneous occupancy.
  In the same certificate, all flights finish before a reset and all next-day
  flights start after release, so the day interval and prefix rows imply the
  timestamp-level exposure rows in \eqref{eq:option_a_opening_timestamp} and
  \eqref{eq:option_a_event_timestamp}. Hence the additional Option A timing,
  capacity and timestamp-hour rows are redundant on the certified class. The
  shared rows remain identical, proving both inclusions.
\end{proof}

The implementation keeps Option A's timing rows and simultaneous occupancy
rows explicitly, while Option B relies on the successful certificate and
ordinary airport-day C10 capacity. Failed certification returns
\texttt{unsupported}; it is not an infeasibility proof. Computational
comparisons on this class must still replay completion, release and objective
reconstruction rather than inferring physical feasibility from projection
identity alone.

\subsection{Flight-hour limits}

The type-A requirement applies to every consecutive period between checks,
including the initial period whose accumulated hours may be nonzero. For
days $1\le s<t\le n_d$, impose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_interval_hours}
  \sum_{r=s+1}^{t}q_{jr}
  \le T^A_{\max}
    +M_{st}(1-y_{js})
    +M_{st}\sum_{r=s+1}^{t-1}y_{jr},
  \qquad \forall j.
\end{equation}
If a check occurs on day $s$ and no check occurs on an intervening day, this
row limits all flight time after that check through day $t$, including flights
that precede a possible check on day $t$. If the row's starting day is not a
check day, or an intervening check resets the counter, the corresponding
window is inactive; a row starting at the most recent check enforces the
limit instead.

For the opening period, impose a separate prefix bound for every day:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_opening_hours}
  \Phi^A_j+\sum_{r=1}^{t}q_{jr}
  \le T^A_{\max}
    +M_{0t}\sum_{r=1}^{t-1}y_{jr},
  \qquad \forall j,\; t=1,\ldots,n_d.
\end{equation}
Only checks on days strictly before $t$ relax this prefix. In particular, a
check after the flights of day $t$ cannot relax the hours accumulated before
that check. Choose finite nonnegative $M_{st}$ and $M_{0t}$ at least as large
as the maximum possible excess over $T^A_{\max}$ in their respective flight
windows. In particular, the opening-period bound must include the initial
accumulated hours: if $U^{\max}_{jt}$ is an upper bound on
$\sum_{r=1}^{t}q_{jr}$, choose
\[
  M_{0t}\ge \max_{j\in\Pc}
  \max\{0,\Phi^A_j+U^{\max}_{jt}-T^A_{\max}\}.
\]
A bound based only on new flight minutes may be too small when
$\Phi^A_j>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness of the corrected A-hour rows]
  \label{prop:a_hour_exactness}
  Suppose resets are executable between the relevant daily flight blocks,
  $q_{jd}\ge0$, $0\le\Phi^A_j\le T^A_{\max}$, and every big-$M$ is at least
  the maximum possible excess in its own window. Then the rows
  \eqref{eq:a_interval_hours}--\eqref{eq:a_opening_hours}, imposed over all
  $1\le s<t\le n_d$ and all $t\in\D$, hold if and only if the physical
  accumulated A exposure is at most $T^A_{\max}$ immediately before every
  possible check, including the opening exposure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  (Sufficiency.) Fix aircraft $j$ and day $t$. If no check occurred on a day
  strictly before $t$, the active opening row bounds
  $\Phi^A_j+\sum_{r=1}^{t}q_{jr}$ and hence the pre-check exposure on day $t$.
  Otherwise let $s<t$ be the last completed check day. The interval row
  $(s,t)$ has no interior reset and therefore bounds
  $\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}q_{jr}$, exactly the physical post-reset exposure.
  Flights on day $t$ are counted before its reset because only earlier checks
  appear in the relaxation sums.

  (Necessity.) Assume the physical exposure rule holds. Any opening row with
  an earlier check is relaxed by a coefficient selected large enough for
  $\Phi^A_j+\sum_{r=1}^{t}q_{jr}-T^A_{\max}$; without an earlier check, its
  left side is exactly the physical exposure. Similarly, an interval row is
  relaxed by a valid $M_{st}$ whenever the start is not a check or an interior
  check exists. If $s$ is a check and no interior check exists, its left side is
  exactly the physical exposure through day $t$ and cannot exceed $T^A_{\max}$.
  Thus every row is satisfied. The two directions prove exactness under the
  stated reset, nonnegativity and valid-big-$M$ assumptions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to Constraint~(13)}

For a fixed aircraft and day pair $(s,t)$, write
\[
  L_{jst}=\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}q_{jr}, \qquad
  Y_{jst}=\sum_{r=s+1}^{t-1}y_{jr}.
\]
The original row has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_c13_original}
  L_{jst}\le T^A_{\max}
    +M_{st}(2-y_{js}-y_{jt})+M_{st}Y_{jst}.
\end{equation}
In \citet{khaled2018compact}, the prose gives the day-pair range
$1\le t-s\le d_{\max}-1$, while displayed equation~(13) uses
$2\le t-s\le d_{\max}-1$ and $s<n_d$. The paper is internally inconsistent
on the lower bound. Our comparison takes the displayed equation as the
formulation actually imposed and reports separately which consecutive-day
gap the prose range would cover.

The row's logic is conditional, not an unconditional limit on every day-pair
window. When both endpoint days are check days and there is no intervening
check, $y_{js}=y_{jt}=1$ and $Y_{jst}=0$, so the row reduces to
$L_{jst}\le T^A_{\max}$. This is the intended check-to-check case, and the
original row is valid for it whenever $(s,t)$ lies in the published index
range. If either endpoint is not a check day, however,
the row receives at least one full $M_{st}$ of slack; if neither endpoint is
a check day, it receives $2M_{st}$. Such a row does not enforce a limit for
that window. That deactivation is appropriate when the pair does not bound
one complete interval between checks.

\paragraph{Coverage gaps of the published rows.}
Because the rows are conditional and restricted in index, four kinds of
exposure period receive no flight-hour bound from Constraint~(13):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the opening period before the first check in the horizon, discussed
  below;
\item consecutive check days, $t=s+1$, where the flights of day $t$ lie
  between two checks (the prose range $1\le t-s$ would cover this case, but
  displayed equation~(13) excludes it);
\item checks exactly $d_{\max}$ days apart: calendar spacing~(12) requires a
  check in every $d_{\max}$ consecutive days and therefore permits this gap,
  while Constraint~(13) stops at $t-s=d_{\max}-1$;
\item the closing period after the last check in the horizon, which has no
  checked end day. A row ending at $t=n_d$ can be indexed when its other
  conditions hold, but it is relaxed when $y_{j,n_d}=0$.
\end{enumerate}
For example, let $d_{\max}=3$, $T^A_{\max}=600$, $\Phi^A_j=0$, and $n_d=6$.
The following aggregate daily patterns violate the limit, yet satisfy~(12)
and every published Constraint~(13) row. They illustrate the hour-row
logic only; they are not claimed to satisfy the flight-trigger placement
constraints, which require an assigned arrival on every check day:
\begin{itemize}
\item consecutive checks: $y_j=(1,1,0,1,0,0)$ and $q_{j2}=700$, with
  zero flight time on the other days, give 700 minutes between the checks on
  days 1 and 2;
\item checks $d_{\max}$ apart: $y_j=(1,0,0,1,0,0)$ and
  $q_{j3}=q_{j4}=400$ give 800 minutes between the checks on days 1 and 4;
\item closing period: $y_j=(1,0,0,1,0,0)$ and $q_{j5}=q_{j6}=400$ give
  800 minutes after the day-4 check.
\end{itemize}
The interval rows~\eqref{eq:a_interval_hours}, imposed for all
$1\le s<t\le n_d$, reject these schedules through the pairs $(1,2)$,
$(1,4)$, and $(4,6)$, respectively. The opening
prefix~\eqref{eq:a_opening_hours} instead covers exposure before the first
check, as illustrated below. The revised
family therefore requires no assumption relating $T^A_{\max}$ to
$d_{\max}$ or to the horizon length.

The opening interval illustrates the physical omission. Let
$T^A_{\max}=600$ minutes, let an aircraft enter with
$\Phi^A_j=10$ minutes already accumulated, and let it fly 595 minutes before
a check at the end of day $t$. The physical exposure immediately before that
check is $10+595=605$ minutes, so the schedule violates the limit. A row
whose start day is not a check day is relaxed by Constraint~(13); a split row
that only checks the new flight time can see $595\le600$ and miss the
violation as well. The opening bound~\eqref{eq:a_opening_hours} instead
compares $\Phi^A_j+595=605$ with 600. Because the check is performed after
day $t$'s flights, $y_{jt}$ must not deactivate this prefix bound.

The alternative two-row endpoint split has a different failure. Its rows
replace $2-y_{js}-y_{jt}$ by $y_{js}$ and $y_{jt}$ separately:
\begin{align}
  L_{jst}&\le T^A_{\max}+M_{st}Y_{jst}+M_{st}y_{js},
    \label{eq:a_split_start}\\
  L_{jst}&\le T^A_{\max}+M_{st}Y_{jst}+M_{st}y_{jt}.
    \label{eq:a_split_end}
\end{align}
If both endpoints are checked and there is no intervening check, both
right-hand sides equal $T^A_{\max}+M_{st}$. This observation alone does not
establish that the full split family accepts a violating schedule: other
windows must also be checked. The following construction verifies all of them.

\paragraph{A complete counterexample.}
Consider one aircraft, indexed by $j=1$, initially at airport $X$, a
five-day horizon, $T^A_{\max}=600$ minutes, $\Phi^A_1=0$, and a minimum
turn time of 30 minutes. Airport $M$ is the only maintenance-capable airport,
with capacity one on each day. Assign all three flights in
\Cref{tab:a_split_witness} to the aircraft. Times are clock times on the
specified day; absolute times add $1440(d-1)$ minutes on day $d$.

\begin{table}[t]

\caption{Flight data for the endpoint-split counterexample. There are no
  flights on days 3 and 5.}
\label{tab:a_split_witness}
\begin{tabular}{ccccccr}
\toprule
Flight & Day & Origin & Destination & Departure & Arrival & Minutes \\
\midrule
1 & 1 & $X$ & $M$ & 08:00 & 09:00 & 60 \\
2 & 2 & $M$ & $N$ & 08:00 & 18:00 & 600 \\
3 & 4 & $N$ & $M$ & 08:00 & 18:00 & 600 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Place checks after flights 1 and 3, on days 1 and 4, respectively. Thus
$x_{i1}=1$ for all three flights, $z_{1,1,1}=z_{3,1,4}=1$, and every other
trigger is zero. The resulting daily data are
\begin{align}
  (q_{11},q_{12},q_{13},q_{14},q_{15})&=(60,600,0,600,0),
    \label{eq:a_split_witness_hours}\\
  (y_{11},y_{12},y_{13},y_{14},y_{15})&=(1,0,0,1,0).
    \label{eq:a_split_witness_checks}
\end{align}
The route $X\to M\to N\to M$ respects continuity and turn times. Both
checks follow assigned arrivals at $M$ on the correct days, have no later
same-day departure, and respect capacity. Hence the routing and
check-placement constraints do not exclude this witness.

Use data-derived interval bounds, including opening prefixes, as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a_split_witness_bounds}
  U_{st}=\sum_{i\in\F:\ s<d_i\le t}\tilde t_i,
  \qquad M_{st}=\max\{0,U_{st}-600\},
  \qquad 0\le s<t\le5.
\end{equation}
These bounds are valid because $U_{st}$ includes every flight in the
corresponding window and $\Phi^A_1=0$. All flights are assigned here, so
$L_{1st}=U_{st}$ for $s\ge1$.

There are ten pairs $1\le s<t\le5$, hence twenty split inequalities.
Only $(1,4)$ and $(1,5)$ have $L_{1st}>600$; both have
$L_{1st}=1200$ and $M_{st}=600$. For $(1,4)$, $Y_{1,1,4}=0$ and both
endpoint indicators equal one, so both split right-hand sides equal 1200.
For $(1,5)$, the day-4 check is interior: $Y_{1,1,5}=1$, $y_{11}=1$, and
$y_{15}=0$. Its two split right-hand sides are 1800 and 1200. Thus these
four inequalities hold. Every other window has $L_{1st}\le600$ and a
right-hand side at least 600, proving that \emph{all twenty} split rows hold.

The opening-prefix inequalities~\eqref{eq:a_opening_hours} also hold:
the day-1 prefix is 60, and every later prefix contains the earlier day-1
check, which supplies the relaxation in \eqref{eq:a_split_witness_bounds}.
The example does not rely on removing the original calendar-spacing
condition either. With $d_{\max}=4$, the two four-day windows $[1,4]$ and
$[2,5]$ each contain a check. Moreover, $(s,t)=(1,4)$ belongs to the
published index range $2\le t-s\le d_{\max}-1$, $s<n_d$ of
Constraint~(13) in \citet{khaled2018compact}.

After the day-1 check, the aircraft accumulates 600 minutes on day 2 and
another 600 on day 4, with no intervening reset. The 1200 minutes before
the day-4 check violate the 600-minute limit. For $(1,4)$, both the original
row~\eqref{eq:a_c13_original} and the revised
row~\eqref{eq:a_interval_hours} impose $1200\le600$ and reject the
schedule. The split therefore fails even when its entire day-pair family,
the opening prefixes, and the check-placement conditions are imposed.
This is a self-contained analytical counterexample, not a computational
audit result.

Conditional on executable resets, the formulation in
\eqref{eq:a_interval_hours}--\eqref{eq:a_opening_hours}
is preferable as a complete representation of the stated flight-hour rule
because each row corresponds to a specific post-check interval or opening
prefix, uses the reset indicator only for checks strictly before the period
being bounded, and counts all flights on the day a check is performed. This
is a coverage and semantics advantage, not a claim that the equations are
automatically stronger in the LP relaxation: that depends on the valid
interval-specific values of $M_{st}$ and $M_{0t}$ and should be measured
separately. The bounds must dominate the maximum possible excess in their
respective windows; choosing them too small can cut off legal schedules, and
choosing them unnecessarily large can weaken the relaxation.
