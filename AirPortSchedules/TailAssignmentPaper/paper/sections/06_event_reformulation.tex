%%==========================================================================
\section{Event-Based Reformulation: Removing the Day Index}
\label{sec:event_reformulation}
%%==========================================================================

\Cref{sec:maintenance,sec:hierarchy} keep the day-indexed structure of
\citet{khaled2018compact}: maintenance state is tracked per calendar day
$d\in\D$, and both the flight-hour recursion~\eqref{eq:c13_state_lower}--%
\eqref{eq:c13_state_bound} and the hierarchy extension repeat this index
for every check type. This section presents an updated, day-free
reformulation of the same C1--C14 requirements. It is the model actually
implemented as \texttt{event\_exact\_state} in \texttt{src/event\_model.py}
and referenced throughout \Cref{sec:maintenance,sec:computational} as the
exact-state formulation; here we give it a complete, self-contained
derivation and prove its correctness directly, rather than only by analogy
to the day-indexed recursion.

\subsection{Modelling principle}
\label{subsec:event_principle}

Every flight already carries an absolute departure and arrival timestamp
measured from the start of the planning horizon, so its calendar day is
derived data, $d_i=\lfloor \tD{i}/1440\rfloor$, not a decision index. Once
this is recognised, the day set $\D$ can be dropped from every maintenance
variable without losing any information: maintenance is represented as a
virtual flight from an airport to itself, starting immediately after a real
flight, occupying the aircraft for a fixed duration, and consuming station
capacity for that duration. A single binary variable $z_{ijk}$ selects check
type $k$ after flight $i$ for aircraft $j$; there is no longer one variable
per (flight, aircraft, \emph{day}, check) tuple, and no separate variable is
needed for a multi-day check's later occupancy days.

\subsection{Additional sets and parameters}

Let $\CH=\{A,B\}$ (flight-hour triggered) and $\CT=\{C,D\}$ (calendar-time
triggered) partition $\C$, matching the split already used in
\Cref{sec:maintenance,sec:hierarchy}. For flight $i$, write $o_i=\aI{i}$,
$a_i=\aD{i}$, and let $p_i=\tI{i}-\tD{i}$ be its flying time (previously
written $\tilde t_i$ in \Cref{sec:maintenance}). For aircraft $j$, let
$a^0_j$ be its initial airport and $r_j$ its initial availability time. Let
$\dt$ be the minimum turn time (as in \Cref{sec:basic}) and $\delta_k$ the
fixed duration of check $k$. Maintenance attached to flight $i$ starts at
$\sigma_i=\tI{i}$ and is the virtual leg
\begin{equation}
  m(i,j,k)=\bigl(a_i,a_i,\sigma_i,\sigma_i+\delta_k\bigr).
  \label{eq:virtual_leg}
\end{equation}
Only flights in $\F^M=\{i\in\F:a_i\in\MA\}$ may be followed by maintenance.
The sparse connection set generalises the arcs implicit in
\eqref{eq:c2}--\eqref{eq:c3}:
\begin{equation}
  \Conn=\bigl\{(i,\ell)\in\F\times\F:
  a_i=o_\ell,\ \tD{\ell}\geq\tI{i}+\dt\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:connection_set}
\end{equation}
For each check type, the connections that leave enough time for that
maintenance event are
\begin{equation}
  \Conn_k=\bigl\{(i,\ell)\in\Conn:
  \tD{\ell}\geq\sigma_i+\delta_k+\dt\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:maintenance_connection_set}
\end{equation}
The aircraft-specific set of feasible first flights is
\begin{equation}
  \F^0_j=\bigl\{i\in\F:o_i=a^0_j,\ \tD{i}\geq r_j\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:first_flight_set}
\end{equation}
Let $T_k$ be the maximum flying time between qualifying checks for
$k\in\CH$ (equal to $T^c_{\max}$ of \Cref{sec:maintenance}), and let $L_k$ be
the maximum elapsed time between qualifying checks for $k\in\CT$, so a limit
of $d_k$ calendar days is stored as $L_k=1440\,d_k$ minutes. Parameters
$\Phi_{jk}$ and $\Psi_{jk}$ are, respectively, the pre-horizon accumulated
flying time and elapsed calendar time (the event-based counterparts of
$\Phi^c_j$ and $\Psi^c_j$ from \Cref{subsec:c13b}). The relation
$k'\succeq k$ means check $k'$ is at least as heavy as $k$, exactly as the
hierarchy order of \Cref{sec:hierarchy}.

\subsection{Decision variables and objective}

\begin{align}
  x_{ij}&\in\{0,1\},
  &&\text{flight $i$ is assigned to aircraft $j$ (as in \Cref{sec:basic})},
  \label{eq:event_x}\\
  w_{i\ell j}&\in\{0,1\},
  &&\text{aircraft $j$ operates $\ell$ immediately after $i$},
  \label{eq:event_w}\\
  z_{ijk}&\in\{0,1\},
  &&\text{aircraft $j$ receives check $k$ immediately after $i$},
  \label{eq:event_z}\\
  u_{ijk}&\geq0,
  &&\text{flying time accumulated after $i$, before its check, } k\in\CH.
  \label{eq:event_u}
\end{align}
Variable $z_{ijk}$ generalises the day-indexed trigger $z_{ijd}$ of
\Cref{sec:maintenance} by replacing the day index $d$ with the triggering
flight $i$ itself -- the day is recoverable as $\lfloor\sigma_i/1440\rfloor$
whenever it is needed for reporting. Likewise $u_{ijk}$ generalises the
per-day state $h^c_{jd}$ of \eqref{eq:c13_state_ab_lower}, indexed by the
flight after which the state is measured instead of by calendar day.
Boundary variables $w_{0ij}$ and $w_{i\omega j}$ indicate, respectively, the
first and last flight of aircraft $j$'s route. The hierarchy-reset indicator
is not stored as a variable; it is the derived expression
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{ijk}=\sum_{k'\in\C:\,k'\succeq k}z_{ijk'},
  \label{eq:event_rho}
\end{equation}
the event-indexed analogue of the hierarchy indicator $\eta_{jdc}$ of
\Cref{sec:hierarchy}.

With assignment costs $c_{ij}$ (as in \eqref{eq:obj}) and maintenance costs
$g_{ijk}$, the objective is
\begin{equation}
  \min\;
  \sum_{i\in\F}\sum_{j\in\Pc}c_{ij}x_{ij}
  +\sum_{i\in\F^M}\sum_{j\in\Pc}\sum_{k\in\C}g_{ijk}z_{ijk}.
  \label{eq:event_objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Event-based constraints C1--C14}

\paragraph{C1 -- Flight coverage.}
Identical to \eqref{eq:c1}:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j\in\Pc}x_{ij}=1,
  \qquad \forall i\in\F.
  \tag{C1}\label{eq:event_c1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{C2 -- Unique predecessor.}
Every assigned flight has one predecessor or is the first route flight:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb 1_{\{\ell\in\F^0_j\}}w_{0\ell j}
  +\sum_{i:(i,\ell)\in\Conn}w_{i\ell j}=x_{\ell j},
  \qquad \forall \ell\in\F,\ j\in\Pc.
  \tag{C2}\label{eq:event_c2}
\end{equation}
Source variables $w_{0\ell j}$ are instantiated only for $\ell\in\F^0_j$;
the indicator is notation for this sparse domain, not an extra variable.

\paragraph{C3 -- Unique successor.}
Every assigned flight has one successor or is the last route flight:
\begin{equation}
  w_{i\omega j}+\sum_{\ell:(i,\ell)\in\Conn}w_{i\ell j}=x_{ij},
  \qquad \forall i\in\F,\ j\in\Pc.
  \tag{C3}\label{eq:event_c3}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{C4 -- One time-ordered route per aircraft.}
\begin{align}
  \sum_{i\in\F}w_{0ij}&\leq1,
  &&\forall j\in\Pc,
  \notag\\
  \sum_{i\in\F}w_{i\omega j}&=\sum_{i\in\F}w_{0ij},
  &&\forall j\in\Pc.
  \tag{C4}\label{eq:event_c4}
\end{align}
Airport continuity, turn time, and the non-overlap correction of
\Cref{sec:basic} are all embedded in $\Conn$: an arc $(i,\ell)$ exists only
if $\ell$ departs from $i$'s arrival airport no earlier than $\tI{i}+\dt$,
which already excludes the simultaneous-departure counterexample of
\Cref{ex:simultaneous_departure_counterexample}. Since every arc moves
forward in time, C2--C4 cannot produce a subtour, so the cyclic-rotation
constraint of the classical time-space formulations is unnecessary here, as
in the base model of \Cref{sec:basic}.

\paragraph{C5 -- Maintenance requires assignment.}
\begin{equation}
  z_{ijk}\leq x_{ij},
  \qquad \forall i\in\F^M,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\C.
  \tag{C5}\label{eq:event_c5}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{C6 -- At most one physical check after a flight.}
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k\in\C}z_{ijk}\leq1,
  \qquad \forall i\in\F^M,\ j\in\Pc.
  \tag{C6}\label{eq:event_c6}
\end{equation}
A heavy check satisfies lighter requirements through $\rho_{ijk}$
(\Cref{eq:event_rho}), so simultaneous A, B, C, and D checks after the same
flight are unnecessary, matching H1--H3 of \Cref{sec:hierarchy}.

\paragraph{C7 -- Maintenance-airport eligibility.}
Variables $z_{ijk}$ are instantiated only for $i\in\F^M$; a dense equivalent
is
\begin{equation}
  z_{ijk}=0,
  \qquad \forall i\in\F\setminus\F^M,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\C.
  \tag{C7}\label{eq:event_c7}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{C8 -- Maintenance duration blocks the next flight.}
An insufficient connection and a maintenance action cannot both be selected:
\begin{equation}
  w_{i\ell j}+z_{ijk}\leq1,
  \qquad \forall(i,\ell)\in\Conn\setminus\Conn_k,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\C.
  \tag{C8}\label{eq:event_c8}
\end{equation}
For a terminal flight, $z_{ijk}$ is instantiated only if $\sigma_i+\delta_k$
falls inside the planning horizon. A multi-day check is therefore one
fixed-duration virtual leg, not one binary variable per occupied day as in
the start/occupancy pair $(s_{jmdc},q_{jmrc})$ of \eqref{eq:c14b}.

\paragraph{C9 -- Continuous-time station capacity.}
Let $b_a(\theta)$ be the number of available maintenance positions at
airport $a$ and time $\theta$, and let $\Theta$ collect all maintenance
start times together with every breakpoint at which $b_a(\theta)$ changes --
the only times at which the left- or right-hand side below can change value:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{i\in\F^M:a_i=a\\ k\in\C:\,\sigma_i\leq\theta<\sigma_i+\delta_k}}
  \sum_{j\in\Pc}z_{ijk}
  \leq b_a(\theta),
  \qquad \forall a\in\MA,\ \theta\in\Theta.
  \tag{C9}\label{eq:event_c9}
\end{equation}
This replaces the day-granular capacity constraint~\eqref{eq:c10} with an
exact continuous-time version; setting $b_a(\theta)=\text{mcap}_{a,d}$ for
$\theta$ inside day $d$ recovers~\eqref{eq:c10} as a special case.

\paragraph{C10 -- Maintenance hierarchy.}
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{ijk}=\sum_{k'\succeq k}z_{ijk'},
  \qquad \forall i\in\F^M,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\C.
  \tag{C10}\label{eq:event_c10}
\end{equation}
This is the event-indexed restatement of H2~\eqref{eq:hier2}; it is a
derived expression, not an extra decision variable, and replaces the
day-indexed hierarchy indicator $\eta_{jdc}$.

\paragraph{C11 -- Flight-hour counter bounds.}
For $k\in\CH$, the counter is active only for an assigned flight and never
exceeds the threshold:
\begin{equation}
  p_i\,x_{ij}\leq u_{ijk}\leq T_k\,x_{ij},
  \qquad \forall i\in\F,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\CH.
  \tag{C11}\label{eq:event_c11}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{C12 -- Flight-hour accumulation and reset.}
For each route connection, let $R_{\ell k}=T_k+p_\ell$ deactivate
propagation when the successor arc is not selected; the reset term needs
only the state bound $T_k$:
\begin{align}
  u_{\ell jk}
  &\geq u_{ijk}+p_\ell-R_{\ell k}(1-w_{i\ell j})-T_k\rho_{ijk},
  \notag\\
  u_{\ell jk}
  &\leq u_{ijk}+p_\ell+R_{\ell k}(1-w_{i\ell j})+T_k\rho_{ijk},
  \notag\\
  u_{\ell jk}
  &\leq p_\ell+T_k\bigl(2-w_{i\ell j}-\rho_{ijk}\bigr),
  \qquad \forall(i,\ell)\in\Conn,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\CH.
  \tag{C12}\label{eq:event_c12}
\end{align}
If $w_{i\ell j}=1$ and no qualifying check follows $i$ ($\rho_{ijk}=0$), the
first two rows give $u_{\ell jk}=u_{ijk}+p_\ell$: pure accumulation. If a
qualifying check follows $i$ ($\rho_{ijk}=1$), the third row together with
C11 gives $u_{\ell jk}=p_\ell$: an exact reset. C12 is therefore the
event-indexed form of the exact recursion
\eqref{eq:c13_state_lower}--\eqref{eq:c13_state_bound} of
\Cref{subsec:c13_state}, propagated along the aircraft's actual selected
route instead of along a fixed calendar-day grid, with no day-pair windows
and no endpoint ambiguity -- it therefore cannot exhibit the split-form
failure mode proved in \Cref{lem:c13_split_limit} and confirmed numerically
in \Cref{subsec:comp_c13}, since there C12 has no "window" that both
endpoints can simultaneously relax: every successor arc is checked
individually.

\paragraph{C13 -- Pre-horizon accumulated flying time.}
For a first-flight arc, the counter is initialised from known aircraft
history, generalising \eqref{eq:c13b}:
\begin{align}
  u_{ijk}&\geq\Phi_{jk}+p_i-(\Phi_{jk}+p_i)(1-w_{0ij}),
  \notag\\
  u_{ijk}&\leq\Phi_{jk}+p_i+(\Phi_{jk}+p_i)(1-w_{0ij}),
  \qquad \forall i\in\F,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\CH.
  \tag{C13}\label{eq:event_c13}
\end{align}
C11--C13 jointly replace the cumulative-flight-hour family
\eqref{eq:c13_paper}--\eqref{eq:c13b} in full, not only its corrected form.

\paragraph{C14 -- Calendar-time spacing without a day set.}
Let $H_0$ and $H_1$ be the horizon boundaries. For $k\in\CT$, the first
deadline is $d^0_{jk}=H_0+L_k-\Psi_{jk}$; if $d^0_{jk}\leq H_1$, require
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{i\in\F^M:\,H_0\leq\sigma_i\leq d^0_{jk}}}\rho_{ijk}\geq1,
  \qquad \forall j\in\Pc,\ k\in\CT.
  \tag{C14a}\label{eq:event_c14a}
\end{equation}
Every selected check whose next deadline lies inside the horizon must be
followed by another qualifying check within $L_k$ time units:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{\ell\in\F^M:\,\sigma_i<\sigma_\ell\leq\sigma_i+L_k}}
  \rho_{\ell jk}\geq\rho_{ijk},
  \qquad \forall i\in\F^M:\sigma_i+L_k\leq H_1,\ j\in\Pc,\ k\in\CT.
  \tag{C14b}\label{eq:event_c14b}
\end{equation}
These timestamp windows are the continuous-time counterpart of the sliding
day window~\eqref{eq:c12_cd}, and give the same due-by-deadline semantics
while the virtual leg~\eqref{eq:virtual_leg} still blocks the aircraft for
the check's full fixed duration.

\subsection{Variables and constraints made obsolete}

The reformulation removes the explicit day index $d$ and every
day-indexed maintenance variable of \Cref{sec:maintenance,sec:hierarchy}:
the trigger $z_{ijd}$ (now $z_{ijk}$), the daily indicator $y_{jdc}$, the
hierarchy indicator $\eta_{jdc}$ (now the derived $\rho_{ijk}$), and the
multi-day start/occupancy pair $s_{jmdc}$/$q_{jmrc}$. It also removes one
binary per occupied day of a multi-day check and every day-pair
accumulation constraint~\eqref{eq:c13_new_a}--\eqref{eq:c13_new_b}. Route
information is now explicit through the sparse successor arcs $w_{i\ell j}$,
which is what lets C12 propagate the flight-hour counter exactly along the
aircraft's actual selected sequence.

\subsection{Complexity and memory reduction}

Let $n_f=|\F|$, $n_p=|\Pc|$, $n_c=|\C|$, $n_d$ be the day-indexed model's
number of planning days, and $n_e=|\Conn|$. The day-indexed maintenance
tensor of \Cref{sec:hierarchy} contains $O(n_pn_dn_c)$ check/hierarchy
binaries plus $O(n_pn_mn_d|\CT|)$ location/occupancy binaries. The
event-based tensor contains only $O(n_fn_pn_c)$ binaries, instantiated
sparsely over $\F^M$:
\begin{equation}
  O\!\left(n_pn_dn_c+n_pn_mn_d|\CT|\right)
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  O\!\left(n_fn_pn_c\right).
  \label{eq:maintenance_reduction}
\end{equation}
The complete event-based model contains
\begin{equation}
  O\!\left(n_fn_p+n_en_p+n_fn_pn_c\right)
  \label{eq:event_complexity}
\end{equation}
binary variables, plus $O(n_fn_p|\CH|)$ continuous counters. The sparse
successor variables add $O(n_en_p)$, but $n_e\ll n_f^2$ in practice because
airport and time incompatibilities eliminate most arcs. Crucially, the
maintenance model no longer scales multiplicatively with the number of
planning days $n_d$, which is the main driver of model size in
\Cref{sec:hierarchy} on long horizons. This variable-count advantage is
confirmed empirically in \Cref{subsec:event_measurements}, but with an
important caveat: constraint~\eqref{eq:event_c12} is itself built over every
arc in $\Conn$, so on dense timetables where $|\Conn|$ grows quickly the
event-based model's \emph{constraint} count can exceed the day-indexed
model's, even while its variable count remains smaller -- a genuine
practical trade-off, not a strict improvement in every dimension.

\begin{remark}
  Removing the day dimension does not remove calendar information: every
  timestamp must remain on one absolute, horizon-wide time scale, or
  overnight gaps and the C/D elapsed-time requirements of C14 cannot be
  recovered. This is why the implementation, \texttt{EventMILPScheduler} in
  \texttt{src/event\_model.py}, keeps the legacy JSON instance format
  unchanged and derives $o_i,a_i,\tD i,\tI i$ directly from it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Correctness}

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:event_correct}
  Assume $\Conn$ contains exactly the physically feasible ordinary
  connections~\eqref{eq:connection_set} and C8 is imposed for every
  maintenance-incompatible connection. Every integral solution of
  C1--C14 above then defines, for each aircraft, a time-feasible route whose
  selected maintenance events satisfy duration, station capacity,
  hierarchy, cumulative flight-hour limits, and calendar-time limits.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  C1 assigns every flight exactly once. C2--C4 chain assigned flights into a
  single time-ordered route per aircraft, with turn-time and non-overlap
  feasibility guaranteed by the construction of $\Conn$
  (\Cref{eq:connection_set}), so no separate non-overlap family analogous to
  \eqref{eq:c4_overlap} is required here. C5--C8 attach each maintenance
  event only to an assigned flight at an eligible station and reserve its
  full duration by blocking every connection that does not leave enough
  time. C9 enforces continuous-time station capacity. C10 applies the
  hierarchy reset exactly as H1--H3 of \Cref{sec:hierarchy}. C11--C13
  propagate and bound the flight-hour counter along the aircraft's actual
  selected route, by the same case analysis as
  \Cref{subsec:c13_state}: whenever $w_{i\ell j}=1$, exactly one of "pure
  accumulation" or "exact reset" applies at every successor arc, so the
  counter can never silently skip a window the way the day-indexed split
  form of \Cref{lem:c13_split_limit} can. C14 links every qualifying
  calendar-time check to its predecessor within the required elapsed time,
  using the known pre-horizon state $\Psi_{jk}$. All stated operating and
  maintenance requirements therefore hold for every aircraft, hence for the
  whole solution.
\end{proof}

This completes, with a full independent derivation, the exact-state
formulation that \Cref{subsec:c13_flaw,subsec:c13_corrected} showed was
needed to repair Constraint~(13): \Cref{prop:event_correct} is the
event-based counterpart of \Cref{thm:physical_feasibility_c1_c14}, extended
from the single type-A check of \Cref{sec:maintenance} to the full
$\{A,B,C,D\}$ hierarchy of \Cref{sec:hierarchy}, without a day index
anywhere in the maintenance constraints.
