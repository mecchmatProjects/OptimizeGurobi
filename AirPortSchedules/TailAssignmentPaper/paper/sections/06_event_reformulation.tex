\section{Ordered Event-Based Reformulation for Type-A Maintenance}
\label{sec:event_reformulation}
%%==========================================================================

The final formulation used in this paper is an ordered event-based model for
type-A maintenance. It removes the day index and does not introduce the
standalone successor-arc event formulation. Flights are processed in
nondecreasing departure-time order, and maintenance is represented by a binary
event attached to the flight that triggers it.

\subsection{Ordered event representation}

For flight $i$, let $o_i$ and $a_i$ be its departure and arrival airports,
$p_i$ its flying time, and $\tI{i}$ and $\tD{i}$ its departure and arrival
timestamps. For aircraft $j$, let $a^0_j$ and $r_j$ be its initial airport and
availability time. A type-A event after flight $i$ is assignment-linked and
occupies the aircraft for duration $\delta_A$ at a maintenance-capable arrival
airport.

Let $(i_1,\ldots,i_{n_f})$ be the flights sorted by
$(\tI{i},i)$. The variables are $x_{irj}$ for flight assignment,
$z_{irj}$ for a type-A event after position $r$, and $q_{rj}\geq0$ for the
cumulative flying time of aircraft $j$ after processing position $r$.
The corrected airport-continuity, turn-time, and overlap constraints remain
active on the assignment variables. In particular, an assignment is allowed
only when it can be connected to the aircraft's preceding assigned flight and
when the required maintenance duration leaves enough time before the next
assigned departure.

The objective is
\begin{equation}
\min\ \sum_{i\in\F,j\in\Pc}c_{ij}x_{ij}
 +\sum_{i\in\F^M,j\in\Pc}g_{ij}z_{ij}.
\label{eq:ordered_event_objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prefix-state propagation}

Let $T_A$ be the maximum flying time between type-A events and let $\Phi_j$
be the pre-horizon accumulated flying time of aircraft $j$. At every ordered
position, an unassigned flight carries the state forward, an assigned flight
adds its duration, and an event resets the state after its triggering flight.
Using valid position-specific big-$M$ values $M_{rj}$, the transition is
represented by
\begin{align}
q_{rj}&\geq q_{r-1,j}-M_{rj}x_{irj},\
q_{rj}&\leq q_{r-1,j}+M_{rj}x_{irj},\
q_{rj}&\geq q_{r-1,j}+p_{i_r}-M_{rj}(1-x_{irj})-M_{rj}z_{irj},\
q_{rj}&\leq q_{r-1,j}+p_{i_r}+M_{rj}(1-x_{irj})+M_{rj}z_{irj},\
q_{rj}&\leq p_{i_r}z_{irj}+T_A(x_{irj}-z_{irj})+M_{rj}(1-x_{irj}),\
q_{r-1,j}+p_{i_r}&\leq T_A+M_{rj}(1-x_{irj}).
\label{eq:ordered_hour_transition}
\end{align}
The first two rows carry the state at an unassigned position. The next two
rows impose accumulation for an assigned flight unless an event is selected.
The fifth row gives the exact post-event state, and the final row prevents a
maintenance event from repairing an arrival that already exceeds $T_A$.
The initial state is $q_{0j}=\Phi_j$.

Events satisfy
\begin{equation}
z_{irj}\leq x_{irj},
\qquad i\in\F^M,\ j\in\Pc,
\label{eq:ordered_event_assignment}
\end{equation}
and timestamp-based incompatibility rows prevent an event from being
followed by a departure that occurs before the maintenance duration and turn
time have elapsed. These rows preserve physical route feasibility while
avoiding a separate successor-variable family.

\begin{proposition}[Ordered prefix-state validity]
\label{prop:ordered_event_correct}
Assume the corrected route constraints define a physically feasible integral
assignment, every event is attached to an assigned flight at a
maintenance-capable airport, and the supplied initial state is valid. Then the
ordered prefix-state formulation satisfies the type-A flight-hour limit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the ordered flight positions. An unassigned position carries the
state unchanged. An assigned position adds its flying time unless an event is
selected, in which case the reset row sets the state to the current flight's
duration. The final transition row and the upper state bound prevent the
threshold from being exceeded before a reset. The induction starts from
$\Phi_j$, so every assigned route satisfies the type-A limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the ordered formulation is the final method}

The ordered formulation combines event-triggered maintenance with a global
flight order and aircraft-local prefix states. It removes the day-pair
accumulation ambiguity of Constraint~(13), avoids the old compact-event
successor-variable formulation, and keeps the physical route constraints
needed for valid tail assignments. The computational study in
\Cref{sec:computational} evaluates this formulation against the legacy A-only
model on matched horizon and fleet-size grids.
