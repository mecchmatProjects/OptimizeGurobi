\subsection{Controlled profile validation}

The first validated run for this branch is a controlled formulation check on
\path{data/validation/option_ab_certified.json}. The instance has one aircraft,
three flights, a three-day horizon, $T^A_{\max}=100$ minutes, a 30-minute
check duration, and protected nightly windows. It is certified for Option~B.
The Khaled and endpoint-split profiles use $d_{\max}=4$, zero-day check
deferral, no opening-hour row, no overlap correction, and the printed A-policy
calendar-spacing domain. The corrected, Option~A, and Option~B profiles use
arrival-day triggers, overlap rows, all interval pairs and opening prefixes,
including initial exposure. This is a shared-input formulation audit, not a
reproduction of the generated or real-data grids in
\citet{khaled2018compact}.

All five runs use CPLEX~12.9.0.0, one thread, presolve on, and a 60-second
wall-clock limit. The driver records input and source hashes, solver settings,
model dimensions, bounds, termination status, and saved decisions. Every
loaded schedule is independently replayed for coverage, continuity, turn
time, maintenance timing, release, capacity and accumulated type-A hours;
Option~A and Option~B use simultaneous-bay replay. The results are summarized
in \Cref{tab:profile_validation}.

The identical objective is a consequence of the chosen fixture: the 100-minute
limit makes a check on each of the three nights necessary, regardless of the
hour-row formulation. It therefore tests implementation identity, replay and
Option~A/Option~B projection on a certified input, not the C13 counterexamples.
On this instance the Option~A and Option~B models have the same assignment,
trigger and hour-row domains and return the same objective. No statistical or
performance conclusion follows from one instance or from sub-second solve
times.

four fleet/horizon cells $(|P|,H)\in\{(3,4),(3,5),(5,4),(5,5)\}$ using
\texttt{experiments/generate\_profile\_timing.py}. The instances have
route-connected spoke--MRO rotations, varied seeded costs, $T^A_{\max}=180$
minutes, a four-day A-calendar spacing limit, and service/protected periods
that certify Option~B. Each generated assignment certificate was independently
replayed before it entered the timing family. All five profiles used identical
JSON inputs within each replicate, CPLEX~12.9.0.0, one thread, presolve on,
the \texttt{early\_check} objective, and a 180-second limit. Each input and
profile output records SHA256 input/source hashes, solver details, bounds,
model size, timings and replay status.

For the direct Option~A/Option~B comparison, all 120 pairs returned the same
objective. A paired two-sided Wilcoxon test on log wall-time ratios was applied
within each fleet/horizon cell, followed by Holm correction across the four
cells. Median A/B ratios were 1.012, 1.038, 1.021 and 1.022, with Holm-adjusted
$p$-values 0.524, 0.157, 0.524 and 0.015. Only the $(5,5)$ cell survives the
four-cell correction, indicating a small A-over-B runtime increase in that
cell, while the other three show no resolved difference. The result is not a
claim of a general speed advantage: solve times are below 0.12 seconds and the
sign varies by cell. It does establish, on this certified class, that the
simplified Option~B constraints preserve the returned objectives and replayed
feasibility under the matched protocol.

The Khaled and split profiles solved all original timing cases optimally, but
their schedules failed strict A-hour replay in 35 and 20 cases, respectively;
their runtimes therefore remain diagnostic rather than feasible-quality
baselines. Raw per-run records and certificates for both the single-run and
repeat studies are stored under \path{results/tables} with these basename
patterns:

\subsection{Scale boundary}

Twelve independently certified scale inputs extended the three exact profiles
to fleet/horizon settings $(10,7)$, $(10,15)$, $(20,7)$ and $(20,15)$, with
three seeded inputs per cell. The 120, 280, 240 and 560 flights in those cells
were solved under the same CPLEX~12.9.0.0, one-thread, 180-second protocol,
giving 36 profile/input runs. All profiles returned the same objective for
optimal inputs and the same incumbent objective for capped inputs.

Thus 27 of 36 scale runs proved optimality and all 36 saved schedules passed
independent replay. The remaining nine runs were all in the 20-aircraft,
15-day cell: each reached its 180-second limit with a replay-passing incumbent
and a substantial nonzero gap. These are feasibility evidence, not optimality
evidence or a claim of scale advantage. A generated 40-aircraft cell was not
solved after this P20/H15 boundary failed to close, so the reported scale claim
stops at 20 aircraft and separates a 15-day incumbent result from solved cases.

\subsection{Heterogeneous repeated cross-solver study}

flight duration and departure timing across four multi-hub cells. Each of the
four inputs was solved three times for every exact profile under CPLEX~12.9 and
HiGHS~1.15, one thread and a 120-second limit, giving 72 runs. All 72

\subsection{Cross-midnight Option A study}

Four Option A-only families use flights that depart shortly before midnight
and arrive after midnight. Option B correctly rejects every input at pre-solve
certification because its protected-period policy requires same-day flights.
Option A was run three times on each input under both solvers, producing 24
rows. All 24 incumbents passed strict replay, including the timestamp-level
A-hour limit. CPLEX proved all 12 solves optimal. HiGHS proved the nine
P4/P10-H4 and P4-H7 runs optimal; its three P10/H7 repeats reached the
120-second limit with the same 18.265\% gap. Their incumbent objectives agreed
with the CPLEX optima within $2.1\times10^{-9}$, so those three HiGHS rows are
cross-solver incumbent evidence, not independent optimality proofs.
\Cref{tab:overnight_repeat} makes that boundary explicit.

\subsection{Unsupported-input control}

The unsupported fixture is
\path{data/validation/option_b_unsupported.json}. It contains a day-1 flight
whose arrival is too late for the declared protected period.
The Option~B pre-solve certificate returns ``unsupported'' with the reason
that the flight is not finished before the protected period. The runner
records zero variables, zero constraints and no incumbent. This is deliberately
not labelled infeasible: the same input need not be rejected by Option~A if an
executable service time exists.

\subsection{Event-model audit status}

The historical event formulation has been recovered from the paperEJOR branch
as an audit-only profile, \texttt{event\_historical\_unverified}. A fixed
schedule in which 120 accumulated minutes exceed a 100-minute A limit before a
check is rejected by an actual E-hour constraint and by independent replay.
This closes the specific known-bad-schedule regression, but it does not
establish full event-model equivalence. Event objectives and timings remain
excluded from result tables until the same route, capacity, calendar and
counterexample battery passes on a wider certified fixture set.

\subsection{Artifacts and limitations}

The five supported outputs are stored as
\path{results/tables/profile_*_20261118.csv}, with matching
\path{.metadata.json}, \path{.schedules.json} and \path{.sources.zip}
sidecars. The unsupported control is stored as
{\small\path{profile_option_b_unsupported_20261118.csv}}.
The versioned submission artifact is built at
\path{results/archive/profile_evidence_20261120/}. It contains the source,
input JSON, result CSVs, sidecars, figures, manuscript, a fixed-timestamp ZIP and
a SHA256 file manifest. A persistent repository deposit and its DOI remain a
submission-time external action; the archive is already content-addressed and
ready for deposit without regenerating results.
The final test baseline is 91 passing tests, including independent certificate,
C13, C8, Option~A,
Option~B and event-counterexample tests. The current experiment still does
not cover heterogeneous or cross-midnight fleets above the reported cells,
fleets above 20 aircraft, real operational disruption data, Gurobi, or broad
hardware-level runtime variability. Synthetic instances remain distinct from
the real-airline-derived study in
\citet{khaled2018compact}; no claim of reproducing that benchmark is made.
