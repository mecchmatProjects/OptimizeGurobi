\section{Conclusions and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%==========================================================================

This paper is restricted to the compact Tail Assignment Problem model and
type-A flight-hour maintenance. The simultaneous-departure example shows that
strict-time balance inequalities need explicit pairwise non-overlap rows to
exclude assignments that cannot be operated by one aircraft. For maintenance,
we distinguish limits between consecutive checks from the opening-period
limit implied by an aircraft's accumulated hours at the start of the horizon,
and show that the published index range of Constraint~(13) also leaves
consecutive-day checks, checks $d_{\max}$ days apart, and the closing period
without a flight-hour bound.
The endpoint-split replacement for Constraint~(13) is invalid: the explicit
counterexample in \Cref{sec:maintenance} satisfies every split row while
violating the flight-hour limit. The interval conditions instead activate
the bound on each check-to-check period and opening prefix.

The computational evidence currently available does not support a reliable
evaluation of the formulation. The instance generator in this
repository creates synthetic data, whereas \citet{khaled2018compact}
generated their test instances from a real airline data set; older tables
also mix formulations and contain
infeasible or incomplete runs. The A-only solver outputs presently in the
workspace are not accompanied by a complete, matching source-and-input
provenance package. Consequently, this manuscript makes no claim to reproduce
the published computational tables or to establish comparative solver
performance.

\subsection*{Future work}

The next step is to implement the stated A-only interval and opening-period
constraints in the executable model, preserve the exact source revision and
input hashes for each run, and independently replay every returned schedule.
Maintenance executability will be addressed through the two proposals in
\Cref{sec:a_executability}: explicit overnight timing constraints and a
protected-period policy certified by restrictive input assumptions. Neither
has yet been implemented or computationally compared. The initial comparison
will use common certified instances; a separate study of irregular and
overnight timetables will assess the restrictions of the assumption-based
approach without conflating different feasible regions with solver performance.
Computational comparisons should then use a feasible, documented instance
family and report solver version, termination status, bounds, runtime, and
validation outcomes.
