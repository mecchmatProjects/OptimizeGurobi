\section{Conclusions and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%==========================================================================

This paper is restricted to the compact Tail Assignment Problem model and
type-A flight-hour maintenance. The simultaneous-departure example shows that
strict-time balance inequalities need explicit pairwise non-overlap rows to
exclude assignments that cannot be operated by one aircraft. For maintenance,
we distinguish limits between consecutive checks from the opening-period
limit implied by an aircraft's accumulated hours at the start of the horizon,
and show that the published index range of Constraint~(13) also leaves
consecutive-day checks, checks $d_{\max}$ days apart, and the closing period
without a flight-hour bound.
The endpoint-split replacement for Constraint~(13) is invalid: the explicit
counterexample in \Cref{sec:maintenance} satisfies every split row while
violating the flight-hour limit. The interval conditions instead activate
the bound on each check-to-check period and opening prefix.

The current computational package is reproducible but deliberately limited.
The executable model now contains named printed-Khaled, endpoint-split,
corrected interval/prefix, Option~A and Option~B profiles. Runs record source
and input hashes, solver configuration, status, bounds, model size and saved
certificates; loaded schedules are independently replayed. The original
40-instance timing family is synthetic, route-connected and small, with
sub-second solves suitable for implementation and projection checks. A
separate scale ladder reaches 20 aircraft and 560 flights: 27 of 36 solves
proved optimality and all 36 incumbents passed replay. Every 20-aircraft,
15-day solve reached the 180-second cap with a 40.49--42.80\% gap, so the
ladder supports a measured feasibility and tractability boundary, not a
large-scale optimality claim. A heterogeneous multi-hub matrix now contributes
72 repeated CPLEX/HiGHS solves, all optimal and replay-passing, with matched
objectives. The cross-midnight Option A matrix contributes 24 replay-passing
runs; CPLEX proves all 12, while HiGHS caps three P10/H7 repeats but returns the
same incumbent objectives. Legacy Khaled/split timings are diagnostic because
their selected schedules often fail strict A-hour replay. The manuscript still
does not reproduce the real-airline-derived computational tables of
\citet{khaled2018compact}.

\subsection*{Future work}

The next evidence steps are a heterogeneous and overnight ladder beyond 20
aircraft, real operational timetables, additional commercial solver backends,
and persistent-repository deposit of the immutable artifact. Option~A and
Option~B have now been
implemented distinctly and compared on certified protected-period inputs:
Option~A retains explicit release conflicts and simultaneous-occupancy rows,
whereas Option~B relies on successful certification plus ordinary daily
capacity. Outside that certified class, failed certification remains
unsupported rather than infeasible. The historical event profile remains
audit-only until it passes the same shared replay and counterexample battery
at scale.
